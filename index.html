01 · Meet the Republic of Portugal

Portugal sits astride the southwestern edge of Europe, facing the vast Atlantic Ocean. Its sunny Mediterranean climate, rich river valleys, and centuries of maritime exploration have nurtured one of Europe's most soulful food cultures.

In Portuguese tradition, a meal is an extended invitation to gather. Crusty rustic bread, rich golden olive oil, garlic, bay leaves, and wine form the heart of the table, while sweet cinnamon, piri-piri, and fresh herbs lift complex dishes.

The Golden Essence

03 · From the Atlantic Table

Three classic Portuguese dishes

Exact measurements and step-by-step instructions — salt cod from Lisboa, green soup from Minho, and the custard tarts of Belém.

Lisboa Prep: 20 mins Serves: 4

Bacalhau à Brás

Shredded Salt Cod with Eggs & Potatoes

Shredded salt cod layered with thin straw potatoes, onions, and scrambled eggs, crowned with black olives and parsley.

Ingredients

  • Salted cod (soaked, desalted, and shredded)
  • Matchstick straw potatoes (batata palha)
  • Large eggs (beaten)
  • Medium yellow onions (thinly sliced)
  • Cloves garlic (minced)
  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • Fresh parsley (finely chopped)
  • Pitted black olives (for garnish)
  • Salt and freshly cracked black pepper

Method

  1. Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add onions and garlic; cook until soft and translucent (about 8–10 minutes).
  2. Add shredded salt cod and sauté for 5 minutes until fully cooked and flaky.
  3. Stir in matchstick potatoes until combined with the cod and onions.
  4. Pour beaten eggs over the mixture. Reduce heat to low and toss gently until eggs are creamy and barely set (do not overcook).
  5. Remove from heat, season with pepper, garnish with fresh parsley and black olives, and serve immediately.
Minho Prep: 15 mins Serves: 6

Caldo Verde

Portuguese Green Soup

A silky potato and kale soup with a slice of chouriço and a drizzle of olive oil — the most beloved bowl in Portugal.

Ingredients

  • Yellow potatoes (peeled and diced)
  • Portuguese chouriço or linguiça sausage (sliced into coins)
  • Collard greens or kale (stems removed, thinly ribboned)
  • Large yellow onion (chopped)
  • Cloves garlic (minced)
  • Water or vegetable broth
  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. In a large stockpot, heat 2 tbsp olive oil. Sauté onion and garlic for 5 minutes.
  2. Add potatoes, water/broth, and 1 tsp salt. Bring to a boil, then simmer for 20 minutes until potatoes are soft.
  3. Puree the pot contents using an immersion blender until smooth and creamy.
  4. Bring soup back to a simmer and stir in thin ribbons of collard greens. Cook uncovered for 5–8 minutes until tender.
  5. In a separate pan, lightly brown sliced chouriço. Stir half into the soup and top individual bowls with remaining slices and a drizzle of raw olive oil.
Belém, Lisboa Prep: 30 mins Serves: 12 tarts

Pastéis de Nata

Portuguese Custard Tarts

Flaky butter crust cradling warm egg custard, blistered on top and dusted with cinnamon — the icon of Portuguese cafés.

Ingredients

  • Puff pastry sheet (store-bought or homemade)
  • Whole milk
  • All-purpose flour
  • Granulated sugar
  • Water
  • Egg yolks
  • Cinnamon stick & 1 strip lemon peel
  • Ground cinnamon and powdered sugar (for dusting)

Method

  1. Whisk flour into 50ml cold milk until smooth. Heat remaining milk with cinnamon stick and lemon peel. Whisk in flour mixture until thickened; remove from heat.
  2. Boil sugar and water together to 100°C (215°F) syrup, then pour in a thin stream into milk mixture. Let cool 10 mins, then whisk in egg yolks. Strain custard.
  3. Roll puff pastry tightly, cut into 12 log rounds, and press into muffin tin cups with wet thumbs to form thin pastry shells.
  4. Fill shells ¾ full with custard. Bake at highest oven temp (250°C / 480°F) for 12–15 mins until pastry is golden and custard tops are charred. Dust with cinnamon.

04 · A Land of Seafaring & Tradition

Portugal at a glance

Atlantic Coast

Sitting at Europe's edge, stretching from the lush green hills of Minho in the north to the sun-soaked beaches of the Algarve in the south.

Seafaring & Tradition

Home to 10 million people with deep ancestral roots in fishing, winemaking, and pastoral farming.

Culture & Language

The world's 6th most spoken language, carrying a spirit of 'Saudade' — deep nostalgic warmth — into daily dining.

The Age of Discovery

Portuguese sailors brought cinnamon, nutmeg, piri-piri peppers, and sugar back to Europe, revolutionizing global desserts and pastries.

Minho

Vinho Verde & Green Valleys

The lush, rain-fed north produces young green wine (Vinho Verde) and grows the kale that stars in caldo verde. Family farms still press their own olive oil here.

Lisboa

Taverns & Pastelarias

Lisbon's cobblestone lanes hum with tasca taverns serving petiscos and the world-famous Pastéis de Belém, baked from a secret 19th-century recipe.

Algarve

Cataplana & Coastal Fire

The southern coast cooks seafood in cataplanas — copper clam-shell pots sealed with bread dough, steaming clams, fish, and piri-piri over open flame.
